# Who These Rules Are For

These are **Spencer Hoberman's** design rules. Read this first so you know whose taste you're applying and why.

## Spencer

- Founder of **SparkTech Studios, Inc.** (Delaware C corp), a studio that builds AI-powered web apps, MVPs and professional-services sites, and runs its own portfolio of products.
- A college student (economics and data science) building several startups at once. UTern is the flagship (applying to YC W27).
- Builds almost everything with AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex and Grok Bot), often several in parallel on different projects. These rules exist so every agent produces work that looks like the same team made it.
- Works fast and hands-off. Gives feedback by looking at the live result or screenshots ("open it for me"), usually as a list of short, direct changes. Expects only the requested changes to be made and everything else left alone.
- Taste in one line: very simple, clean, professional, "Apple/Linear-grade". The fastest way to lose Spencer is anything that looks generic or AI-made.

## How Spencer's taste formed

The rules come from a review of ~40 of Spencer's projects plus hundreds of Spencer's own design messages to AI agents (Aug–Oct 2026), corrected by Spencer line by line. Recurring lessons:

- Spencer repeatedly rejected the generic "AI designer" default (cream paper, serif headline, terracotta accent, italic accent words) and chose white, ink and one clean sans.
- The most repeated complaints were: things looking "generic" or "AI", 01/02/03 numbering, tags above titles, pill tags, boxy layouts, too many words, repeated statements, off-center or overlapping elements on phones, logos with backgrounds around them, and arrows in buttons.
- Full redesigns Spencer didn't ask for usually got reverted ("I like more how it currently looks"). "Make it less AI" means remove the AI tells, not invent a new identity.
